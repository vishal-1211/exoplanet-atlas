What is coming next in the search for planets

The catalogue grows every week, and the next few years bring instruments built for new kinds of discovery. Dates are from the agencies' own announcements as of October 2026 and can change.

Launched August 2026

Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope

NASA's wide-field telescope launched on 30 August 2026 on a Falcon Heavy. It is travelling to a point about 1.5 million km from Earth, a journey of around three months, followed by about three months of testing. First public images are targeted for early 2027. For planets it will watch millions of stars towards the centre of the Milky Way for the brief brightening caused by gravitational microlensing, which can find planets that other methods cannot, and it carries a coronagraph to test blocking starlight to photograph planets.

Planned: March 2027

PLATO

ESA's mission has 26 cameras and will stare at huge patches of sky for years. Its goal is to find Earth-sized planets in the habitable zone of Sun-like stars, measure their sizes, and use tiny changes in starlight to learn the stars' ages. It is planned to launch on an Ariane 6 rocket.

ESA's PLATO page ↗

Planned: 2029

Ariel

ESA's Ariel will not look for new planets. It will study the atmospheres of about 1,000 known ones, from rocky worlds to gas giants, to make a chemical census. It will sit at the same distant point as Webb and scan the whole sky roughly every three months.

Operating now

Webb, TESS and CHEOPS

The James Webb Space Telescope reads the air of planets as starlight passes through it. TESS keeps surveying nearly the whole sky for transits and feeds the candidate list. ESA's CHEOPS measures the exact sizes of planets already known. All three keep adding to the catalogue.

Data from Gaia

Gaia's star wobbles

ESA's Gaia finished observing and was retired in 2025, but its measurements of tiny star movements are still being turned into planet discoveries. Astronomers expect its later data releases to reveal many giant planets that tug their stars sideways. Check ESA for the release date.

Concept: no launch date

Habitable Worlds Observatory

NASA's plan for a large space telescope built to photograph Earth-sized planets around Sun-like stars and search their air for gases that could signal life. It is still being designed, and no launch date is set. Very large ground telescopes in Chile are also due late this decade.
