HR 8799 c

HR 8799 c is a gas giant exoplanet 135 light-years away, orbiting HR 8799. Discovered in 2008 by imaging. A year there lasts 188.9 years. It is 13 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One of four giant planets photographed directly around the young star HR 8799, among the first multi-planet systems ever seen in pictures.
  • One year here lasts 188.9 years, so a single orbit takes 188.9 times longer than Earth's.
  • It is 134.5 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 135 years ago.
  • Its radius is 13× Earth's radius, or 1.16× Jupiter's.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.64% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • It weighs 9.44× Jupiter in mass (3,000× Earth).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 1,200 K (927 °C), above the boiling point of water at sea level on Earth.
  • It receives only 0.34% of the starlight Earth gets.
  • It orbits at 38 AU, farther from its star than Neptune is from the Sun (30 AU).
