HD 106515 A b

HD 106515 A b is a super-jupiter exoplanet 111 light-years away, orbiting HD 106515 A. Discovered in 2012 by radial velocity. A year there lasts 9.9 years. It is 12.1 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One year here lasts 9.9 years, so a single orbit takes 9.9 times longer than Earth's.
  • It is 111.2 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 111 years ago.
  • Its radius is 12.1× Earth's radius, or 1.08× Jupiter's.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 1.48% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • At 18.9× Jupiter's mass, it is heavy enough that it may really be a brown dwarf (a failed star).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 117 K (-157 °C), below the freezing point of water.
  • It receives only 3.4% of the starlight Earth gets.
  • Its orbit is a stretched oval (eccentricity 0.57). It is 4 times farther from its star at the farthest point than at the closest, so its seasons are extreme.
