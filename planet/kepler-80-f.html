Kepler-80 f

Kepler-80 f is a super-jupiter exoplanet 1,205 light-years away, orbiting Kepler-80. Discovered in 2016 by transit. A year there lasts 23.7 hours. It is 1.21 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • A year here is shorter than an Earth day. It lasts just 23.7 hours.
  • It is 1,205 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 1,205 years ago.
  • Its radius is 1.21× Earth's radius.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.03% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • At 14× Jupiter's mass, it is heavy enough that it may really be a brown dwarf (a failed star).
  • If it has a solid surface, gravity there would be about 3,046× Earth's, so a 70 kg person would feel like 213,224 kg.
  • Its average density is 13,871 g/cm³, denser than Earth. It may have an unusually large metal core.
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 1,054 K (781 °C), above the boiling point of water at sea level on Earth.
