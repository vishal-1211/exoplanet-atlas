L 98-59 f

L 98-59 f is a super-earth exoplanet 35 light-years away, orbiting L 98-59. Discovered in 2025 by radial velocity. A year there lasts 23.1 days. It is 1.48 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One year here lasts 23.1 days, so about 15.8 of its years fit into a single Earth year.
  • It is 34.6 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 35 years ago.
  • Its radius is 1.48× Earth's radius.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.18% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • It is 2.8× Earth's mass.
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 285 K (12 °C), between the freezing and boiling points of water on Earth. Whether it actually has liquid water depends on an atmosphere we do not know about.
  • It orbits at 0.105 AU, closer to its star than Mercury is to the Sun (0.39 AU).
  • It orbits where starlight is in a range that could allow liquid water on a rocky planet. That does not mean it is habitable: its atmosphere and surface are unknown.
