HD 156279 b

HD 156279 b is a gas giant exoplanet 118 light-years away, orbiting HD 156279. Discovered in 2011 by radial velocity. A year there lasts 133.4 days. It is 12.5 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One year here lasts 133.4 days, so about 2.7 of its years fit into a single Earth year.
  • It is 118.1 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 118 years ago.
  • Its radius is 12.5× Earth's radius, or 1.12× Jupiter's.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 1.45% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • It weighs 9.43× Jupiter in mass (2,998× Earth).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 362 K (89 °C), between the freezing and boiling points of water on Earth. Whether it actually has liquid water depends on an atmosphere we do not know about.
  • It receives 2.8 times as much starlight as Earth does.
  • Its orbit is a stretched oval (eccentricity 0.65). It is 5 times farther from its star at the farthest point than at the closest, so its seasons are extreme.
