HAT-P-13 c

HAT-P-13 c is a super-jupiter exoplanet 805 light-years away, orbiting HAT-P-13. Discovered in 2009 by radial velocity. A year there lasts 1.2 years. It is 12.3 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One year here lasts 1.2 years, similar to an Earth year.
  • It is 805 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 805 years ago.
  • Its radius is 12.3× Earth's radius, or 1.1× Jupiter's.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.41% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • At 14.3× Jupiter's mass, it is heavy enough that it may really be a brown dwarf (a failed star).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 340 K (67 °C), between the freezing and boiling points of water on Earth. Whether it actually has liquid water depends on an atmosphere we do not know about.
  • Its orbit is a stretched oval (eccentricity 0.66). It is 5 times farther from its star at the farthest point than at the closest, so its seasons are extreme.
  • It orbits HAT-P-13, a Sun-like yellow (G) star at 5,653 K.
