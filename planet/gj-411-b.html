GJ 411 b

GJ 411 b is a super-earth exoplanet 19 light-years away, orbiting GJ 411. Discovered in 2019 by radial velocity. A year there lasts 12.9 days. It is 1.45 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One year here lasts 12.9 days, so about 28.2 of its years fit into a single Earth year.
  • It is 18.5 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 19 years ago. That makes it the 50th closest of the 6,445 known planets.
  • Its radius is 1.45× Earth's radius.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.13% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • It is 2.69× Earth's mass.
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 350 K (77 °C), between the freezing and boiling points of water on Earth. Whether it actually has liquid water depends on an atmosphere we do not know about.
  • It receives 3.1 times as much starlight as Earth does.
  • It orbits at 0.079 AU, closer to its star than Mercury is to the Sun (0.39 AU).
