HD 284149 AB b

HD 284149 AB b is a super-jupiter exoplanet 384 light-years away, orbiting HD 284149 A. Discovered in 2017 by imaging. A year there lasts 8,422 years. It is 12 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One year here lasts 8,422 years, so a single orbit takes 8,422 times longer than Earth's.
  • It is 384.2 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 384 years ago.
  • Its radius is 12× Earth's radius, or 1.07× Jupiter's.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.65% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • At 26.3× Jupiter's mass, it is heavy enough that it may really be a brown dwarf (a failed star).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 2,395 K (2,122 °C), hot enough to melt iron (1,811 K).
  • It receives only 0% of the starlight Earth gets.
  • It orbits at 431 AU, farther from its star than Neptune is from the Sun (30 AU).
