ZTF J1230-2655 b

ZTF J1230-2655 b is a super-jupiter exoplanet 609 light-years away, orbiting ZTF J1230-2655. Discovered in 2025 by transit. A year there lasts 5.7 hours. It is 13.79 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • A year here is shorter than an Earth day. It lasts just 5.7 hours.
  • It is 608.9 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 609 years ago.
  • Its radius is 13.8× Earth's radius, or 1.23× Jupiter's.
  • At 22.1× Jupiter's mass, it is heavy enough that it may really be a brown dwarf (a failed star).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 2,950 K (2,677 °C), hot enough to melt iron (1,811 K).
  • It receives about 17,890 times as much starlight as Earth does.
  • It orbits at 0.0065 AU, closer to its star than Mercury is to the Sun (0.39 AU).
  • A planet this close to its star is probably tidally locked, with one side always facing the star, like the Moon facing Earth.
