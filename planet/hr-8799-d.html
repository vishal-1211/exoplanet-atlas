HR 8799 d

HR 8799 d is a gas giant exoplanet 135 light-years away, orbiting HR 8799. Discovered in 2008 by imaging. A year there lasts 101.3 years. It is 13 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One of four giant planets photographed directly around the young star HR 8799, among the first multi-planet systems ever seen in pictures.
  • One year here lasts 101.3 years, so a single orbit takes 101.3 times longer than Earth's.
  • It is 134.5 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 135 years ago.
  • Its radius is 13× Earth's radius, or 1.16× Jupiter's.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.64% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • It weighs 9.44× Jupiter in mass (3,000× Earth).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 1,300 K (1,027 °C), above the boiling point of water at sea level on Earth.
  • It receives only 0.85% of the starlight Earth gets.
  • Its orbit is a stretched oval (eccentricity 0.6). It is 4 times farther from its star at the farthest point than at the closest, so its seasons are extreme.
