HIP 97233 b

HIP 97233 b is a super-jupiter exoplanet 334 light-years away, orbiting HIP 97233. Discovered in 2014 by radial velocity. A year there lasts 2.9 years. It is 12.1 times the size of Earth.

Interesting facts

  • One year here lasts 2.9 years, so a single orbit takes 2.9 times longer than Earth's.
  • It is 333.6 light-years away. The light we see from its star left 334 years ago.
  • Its radius is 12.1× Earth's radius, or 1.08× Jupiter's.
  • Seen edge-on, it would block about 0.04% of its star's light as it passes in front.
  • At 19.2× Jupiter's mass, it is heavy enough that it may really be a brown dwarf (a failed star).
  • Its equilibrium temperature is 354 K (81 °C), between the freezing and boiling points of water on Earth. Whether it actually has liquid water depends on an atmosphere we do not know about.
  • It receives 2.6 times as much starlight as Earth does.
  • Its orbit is a stretched oval (eccentricity 0.63). It is 4 times farther from its star at the farthest point than at the closest, so its seasons are extreme.
