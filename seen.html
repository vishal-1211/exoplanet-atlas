Planets we have seen, and atmospheres we have studied

Nearly all exoplanets are found indirectly, from what they do to their star. A few are bright enough and far enough from their star to photograph directly. Others have had their air analysed by starlight filtered through it. This page lists both.

Atmospheres astronomers have studied

When a planet crosses its star, a little starlight passes through the planet’s air. Gases take out particular colours, so a spectrum reveals what is there. These are well-known examples. This list is written by hand, not taken from the catalogue data, so check the telescope teams’ own pages for the latest results.

HD 209458 bHubble, 2001–2003The first planet seen crossing its star (1999) was also the first to have an atmosphere detected: sodium in 2001, and later a huge cloud of hydrogen streaming away from it.HD 189733 bHubble, 2013Its colour was measured from reflected light: a deep blue. The blue is thought to come from glassy particles in its air, not oceans. Water vapour has also been detected.WASP-39 bJWST, 2022–2023Webb found carbon dioxide in its atmosphere, the first clear detection of that gas on a planet around another star, and later sulphur dioxide, made by sunlight acting on the air.WASP-96 bJWST, 2022Webb’s first full spectrum of an exoplanet showed the fingerprint of water vapour and signs of clouds.WASP-76 bVLT (ESPRESSO), 2020Iron vapour was detected on its hot day side and not on its cooler night side, which fits iron condensing out and falling as rain. This one was measured from the ground.KELT-9 bGround and HubbleHotter than most stars’ surfaces, its day side is so hot that molecules break apart. Atoms of iron and titanium have been detected in its air.K2-18 bJWST, 2023Webb found methane and carbon dioxide in the air of this planet in its star’s habitable zone. A claimed signal of a sulphur molecule is disputed. Nobody has found evidence of life.TRAPPIST-1 bJWST, 2023Webb measured its heat and found no sign of a thick atmosphere. It looks like bare, hot rock. The next planet out, TRAPPIST-1 c, also shows no thick carbon-dioxide air.55 Cnc eJWST, 2024A lava world. Webb saw changes in its heat that may mean a thick atmosphere of gases from the rocky interior, but this is still being worked out.
